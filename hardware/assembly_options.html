Claude Code Companion Peripheral · estimated 2026-07-02 · all figures are estimates (±25–30%), not quotes — JLCPCB/PCBWay block automated fetches, so prices come from indexed catalog data and typical current rates. A live JLCPCB quote upload is the only way to firm these up.

The surprise finding: full turnkey costs about the same as soldering the switches yourself. Buying switches retail (plus a second shipment) ≈ what JLCPCB charges to solder them for you. So the middle options earn their place as contingency plans (e.g., if the XIAO module listing is out of stock), not as savings.

A · Full DIY $115–145

5 bare PCBs + every part bought retail; you solder all ~17 components per board.

⚠ hot-air/hotplate required  Effort

You do

  • Reflow the SPH0645 mic — bottom-port LGA, no iron-accessible leads; this is the yield risk
  • Iron-solder XIAO castellations, slide, LED, passives, switches
  • 8–15 h total for 5 boards

Where the money goes

  • PCBs ~$10–18 · parts ~$90 retail (XIAO $5.40, mic $2.40, switches+caps ~$9/bd)
  • 2–3 supplier shipments $15–25
  • +$40–120 hot-air station if you don't own one

Cheapest in dollars, most expensive in hours and risk. Only sensible if you want the build experience itself.

B · Vendor does all SMD, you do the switches $180–225

JLCPCB assembles mic, LED, slide, passives, XIAO. You solder 5 Choc switches per board.

✓ beginner-level iron work  Effort

You do

  • 10 big through-hole joints per board (~15–20 min each board)
  • Order switches + caps retail (~$45–55 incl. shipping — that add-on is why B ≈ D in price)

Vendor does

  • All the hard parts: mic reflow, module placement, SMD
  • Fees: setup+stencil ~$33, ~9 part lines × $1.50, possible XIAO fixture fee

Worth choosing only if you want switch-choice flexibility (try different Choc weights) or the THT quote comes back ugly.

C · Vendor does everything except the XIAO $150–190

The contingency plan if the XIAO's "New Arrivals" library listing is out of stock or its fixture fee is disproportionate. You buy modules from Seeed ($5.40) and solder them on.

△ edge-pad soldering, moderate  Effort

  • 14 castellated joints per board with a regular iron — exposed edge pads, very forgiving compared to the mic; ~10 min/board
  • Switches, mic, everything else arrives soldered
  • Skips the fixture fee and the least-proven library listing
Recommended

D · Full turnkey — zero soldering $175–225

JLCPCB assembles every component including the through-hole switches (their wave/hand-solder service) and the XIAO module. Every BOM part already has a JLCPCB library listing.

✓ zero solder  Effort

You do

  • Press 5 keycaps on (friction fit)
  • Drive 4 screws at enclosure time

Cost anatomy (qty 5)

  • Parts ~$80 · PCB ~$15 · fees ~$55–75 (setup, stencil, loading, THT, fixture)
  • + keycaps ~$12 from a keyboard shop (not in JLC's library)

Qty note: fixed fees are ~$60–75 regardless of count. At qty 2 that's ~45% of the bill (~$55/board); at qty 5 it dilutes to ~$40/board. Order 5.

E · Whole device from one vendor $210–280

Option D plus 5 sets of enclosure shells from JLC3DP, in one combined JLCPCB checkout. The STL/STEP files are already in the repo (hardware/enclosure/output/).

✓ zero solder, one order  Effort

  • Shell pairs est. $5–12 each in gray resin or PA12 (nicer than home FDM for this size)
  • Your entire build: 4 screws + 5 keycaps per device
  • Only outside purchase remains the keycaps

Sensible after the layout sign-off (issue #12) and one cheap fit-check print — don't buy 5 shell sets against an unverified fit.

Caveats & the one real next step

  • Routing doesn't change this math — assembly cost keys off board size, layer count, and part count, all fixed. The board is placed + netlisted; ~30 min of routing in KiCad (issue #13) is required before any of B–E can actually be ordered, but not for choosing between them.
  • The XIAO (C9900176459) and Choc switch (C9900088831) are "New Arrivals" library entries — stock, price, and the module's "assembly fixture" surcharge are unverified. This is the swing variable between B/C/D.
  • Keycaps (MBK blanks) are not in any assembly library — a small keyboard-shop order (~$12) exists in every option.
  • Every dollar figure here is an estimate from catalog snippets; the decision-grade version is uploading the routed board + BOM to JLCPCB's quote tool and reading real numbers off the screen.

Bottom line: pick D (or E once the enclosure is fit-checked). The zero-solder premium over doing the switch soldering yourself is roughly zero, and A only wins if the hours and the hot-air work are the point.
